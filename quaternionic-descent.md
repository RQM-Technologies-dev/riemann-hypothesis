# Quaternionic descent of a paired theta index

This is a paper-level reduction for invertible theta blocks in the quotient-metric part of Alpöge’s argument. It requires no assumption about zeta zeros. No replacement of the full Lean proof has been formalized.

## The identity

Let K be a number field with a nontrivial involution, write F for its fixed field, and write w* for the conjugate of a finite place w. Let θ:V→W be an isomorphism of d-dimensional K-spaces, H a nondegenerate Hermitian form on V, and T and M full integral lattices in V and W. Put L=θT and transport H to h on W by θ inverse. For lattices A,B in the same space let i_w(A,B) be the local length of A/(A∩B) minus that of B/(A∩B), and j_w(h,A)=i_w(A,A^#), with the integral Hermitian dual. Then

    i_w(M,L) + i_w*(M,L) + j_w(H,T) = j_w(h,M).

Indeed, signed index is additive along lattice chains. Hermitian duality reverses local relative index and exchanges conjugate places, giving i_w(L^#,M^#)=i_w*(M,L). This follows by dualizing the elementary divisors over the conjugate discrete valuation ring. The map θ identifies T and its dual with L and its h-dual. Add the three indices along M → L → L^# → M^#. At a fixed place both contributions occur, giving twice the same index. The statement includes fractional lattices, for which the indices can be negative.

In a basis m of M valid at both conjugate places, the result is minus the w-valuation of the determinant of the h-Gram matrix of m. One may choose a basis over the semilocal ring at the pair, since a full lattice is free there. No global freeness is required.

## The quaternion algebra

Take B=(K/F,−1)=K⊕KJ with J²=−1 and Jz=conjugate(z)J. Embedding GL_d(K) in GL_d(B), the reduced norm in the central simple algebra M_d(B) of a transition matrix S in GL_d(K) is det(S) conjugate(det(S)). Over the splitting field K the two diagonal blocks are S and its conjugate. Its valuation accounts for the conjugate index pair. At a complex place of K over a real place of F the real algebra is Hamilton's quaternions. The argument itself works by determinant-line duality; it does not require a quaternionic action on the original theta spaces.

## Exact check at a split prime

Take K=Q(i), w=(2+i), M=Z[i], H=1 and θ multiplication by π=2+i. Let T=π^k M for any integer k. The two image indices are k+1 and 0. The source dual is conjugate(π)^(-k) M and its defect at w is −k. The sum is 1 for every k. The transported Gram scalar is 1/5, whose negative w-valuation is 1. For arbitrary θ the defect can be large even when T disappears from the expression.

The square invertible hypotheses and the three-term local quantity occur in [StepB](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/712a6aef8fe265fb6f3a5fa1f3101a017aec8fe9/src/Formalism/QuotientMetricDivisors/Statement.lean#L157). The source already proves a [dual-index/Gram identity](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/712a6aef8fe265fb6f3a5fa1f3101a017aec8fe9/src/Formalism/QuotientMetricDivisors/HermitianTwistDivisors/RatHerm.lean#L150) and [assembles the paired terms](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/712a6aef8fe265fb6f3a5fa1f3101a017aec8fe9/src/Formalism/QuotientMetricDivisors/HermitianTwistDivisors/ReductionSecondStep.lean#L695). Other stages have rectangular theta maps or need estimates on cuts by subspaces. Their hypotheses must be handled separately.

For genuine proof compression, one must bound the transported target discriminants in the same weighted global sum, for the same family, twists and window, with a loss within the existing budget. An identity for that sum does not supply its bound. Likewise the full determinant does not control the elementary divisors on subspaces: diag(π^k,π^(-k)) has determinant 1 for every k. The subspace problem requires finer data, such as determinantal ideals of minors. The analytic quaternionic reformulation via the intrinsic slice extension gives a corollary of the verified classical theorem; by itself it makes no reduction in those arithmetic dependencies.

## A sharp local test for every lower-dimensional cut

Let O be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer π, and let the columns
of A generate a full sublattice L of M=O^n. A may be rectangular.
Write d_k(A) for the valuation of the ideal generated by its k-rowed
minors, with d_0=0. Put Q=M/L and take an integer e≥0. Then

    R_e = length(π^e Q) = d_n(A) − d_n([A | π^e I])
        = max_{0≤k≤n} [d_n(A) − d_{n−k}(A) − ke].

This is exactly the smallest nonnegative r such that for every fraction-field
subspace W the length of (M∩W)/(L∩W) is at most e dim(W)+r.
Indeed the cut quotient embeds as a submodule B of Q generated by at
most dim(W) elements. The length of B/π^e B is at most e dim(W);
the length of π^e B is at most the length of π^e Q. In Smith coordinates
with exponents s_1≤...≤s_n, choose W spanned by the axes with s_i>e.
This realizes excess R_e=Σ max(s_i−e,0). Augmenting A by π^e I
replaces each Smith exponent by min(s_i,e), proving the formula.
For fixed k, choosing the k largest axes gives the sharp cut length
d_n(A)−d_{n−k}(A); the upper bound also follows by taking e=s_{n−k}
in the preceding argument when 0<k<n. The endpoints are immediate.

The formula applies directly to the localized ambient integral theta
sublattice in the all-subspace estimate. At each actual family point
the existing estimate implies R_e≤r_v, so replacing the residual by
R_e preserves its weighted budget and threshold. This does not identify
that ambient presentation with the later projected row theta matrix.
The maps and denominators between these stages remain part of the proof.

## Split-place obstruction even with every individual minor norm

Let K=Q(i), π=2+i, M=Z[i]^2, and k≥1. Compare A=5^k I with
B=diag(π^(2k),conjugate(π)^(2k)). Both are integral, both have the same
determinant 5^(2k), both have the same Gram matrix 5^(2k) I, and the
field norms of all corresponding minors agree. With standard source
metric their transported target metrics are both 5^(−2k) I. At w=(π)
the Smith exponents are (k,k) and (0,2k); at the conjugate place the
second matrix exchanges the axes. At depth k the residual is 0 versus
k at each place. In the second example the ideal generated by the
entries is the unit ideal even though each nonzero entry has norm
5^(2k). Taking the norm of the ideal of minors is different from taking
the ideal of the norms of individual minors.

## Where the discriminant estimate can enter

[ONwith](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/712a6aef8fe265fb6f3a5fa1f3101a017aec8fe9/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteForms/IndefiniteTree/ExampleSatisfiesConditionsAtFive.lean#L360)
bounds the paired image indices separately from the source duality
defect. [boundsMu](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/712a6aef8fe265fb6f3a5fa1f3101a017aec8fe9/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteForms/IndefUse.lean#L271)
combines those estimates. In its notation, the normalized weighted
sum of transported defects over twisted places and rows is bounded by

    2 Σ_{j,w} lw(w) ramw(w) F0(w,j) + Σ_j (E1(j)+E2(j)) + E3.

This follows by adding the ON image inequality and the row dual-defect
inequality, dividing by D, and invoking their three existing weighted
error budgets. It is a consequence on the original window. To bypass
this assembly arithmetically one needs an independent proof of the
displayed bound or of the requisite weighted R_e estimate together with
the projector and duality controls. A reduced norm by itself does not
give either proof. No Lean dependency has been removed in this audit.

## Direct passage through a row projector

Fix an actual family point and row. Localize at a DVR, let Q=M/L for the
P-stable theta lattice L, and let B=(M∩W)/(L∩W) for the character
eigenspace W. Write δ=[P:U], u=v(|U|), and R_e(Q)=length(π^e Q).
The integral character sum D on W acts by |U| and kills all other
coset translates in the Mackey orthogonality identity. For representatives
g_i of the cosets, use the maps

    S((b_i)) = Σ_i g_i b_i,       T(q)_i = D g_i^(-1) q.

Both descend to Q and B because M,L are stable. Their composition
TS on B^δ is |U| times the identity. Applying this to π^e B and taking
lengths of images proves

    δ R_(e+u)(B) ≤ R_e(Q).

This form retains the subgroup-index saving by shifting depth. The
same inequality holds on a further subspace of W by injection. For
Z=W∩V₀ of rank d, length of the cut quotient is consequently at most
(e+u)d+R_e(Q)/δ. Source inclusions Θ⊆σΘ_K and |U|(Θ_K∩W)⊆Θ₀
allow scaling the cut of Θ by |U|/σ inside the cut of Θ₀. This adds
(u−v(σ))d to the signed index. The idempotent e₀ onto V₀ fixes the
cut, so its image of Θ₀ contains that cut. Retaining the original twist,
image comparison and transport adds the same ramification-weighted error
as before. The resulting row bound is

    I_v ≤ (E−v(σ)+2u)d + R_E(Q)/δ + ram_v ε₀.

It is at least as strong as the source inequality: δd ≤ δ dim W ≤ n
by Mackey orthogonality in the ambient n-dimensional space, and
R_E(Q) ≤ r_v. The savings against the displayed source bound, with
the same data and normalization, is (n/δ−d)u+(r_v−R_E(Q))/δ.

For interior rows the source's localStepQ already bounds R_e before
passing to all cuts: its hLA' is precisely length((π^e M+Θ)/Θ) ≤
L1+L2+L3+q. The four terms are the pole, base, coefficient-lattice,
and spanning defects. Their geometric bounds and weighted summation
are still used. On extending DVRs with ramification index h, Smith
exponents multiply by h; at depth E≥he the defect grows by at most
h. Enlarging the target theta lattice decreases it by quotient
monotonicity. In the actual transfer the intersection with the cusp
subspace injects the corresponding scaled quotient in the ambient
one, with the source's comparison between local forms and their
completion. Keep the terminal row's separate endpoint estimate.

This derivation bypasses the switch from excess to arbitrary cuts and
back at the projector on paper. The full Lean dependency graph has
not been refactored or replayed. Geometric height summation, two-run
selection, descent, and the final theta-image comparison still enter.
